Team
Moodboard

A team activity where everyone fills in a fun questionnaire, and the answers are revealed one by one during the session. The team guesses who each statement belongs to — then hits reveal.

What is it? Eight questions about how you work, what animal you are in focus mode, your power anthem, and a few honest check-ins. During the session, the team reads AI-generated mystery statements and guesses who is who before the big reveal. A team heatmap then shows collective patterns to spark a real conversation.

The prompts that shaped it
Key Conversations with Claude
The spark
"We want to do a team activity where I ask in advance some things from my colleagues and then present them at the activity — something like an employee moodboard that defines them."
Result: The entire concept — 8 questions, 9 animals, the reveal flow — designed from this single message.
Going FanDuel-only
"I want only FanDuel tools — nothing out on the internet. And at the end I want a heatmap page that tells us something about the entire team."
Result: Redirected the entire architecture. Claude spent the next phase navigating every IT block until finding the Slack webhook solution.
The heatmap direction
"I'd love it a bit more dashboardy and without picking on individuals — as this is something we'll do when we're all in the same room."
Result: Rebuilt the heatmap as aggregate-only, first-person plural, with conversation starters instead of individual analysis.
The statements
"The statements are pretty much a copy paste of their entry — I'd like your analysis on it. No genders. No answers revealed. Just the meaning behind their answers."
Result: All 13 statements rewritten as psychological reads — what the combination of choices reveals, not what the choices were.
Tips for Running the Session
→Give the team 3–5 days to fill in the form before the session.
→Keep the AI statements a mystery — don't share them in advance.
→Use the heatmap after the reveals, not before — it lands better when people already know each other's answers.
→Play the power anthem on Spotify or YouTube for the full effect on reveal.
→The whole session runs in 60–90 minutes for a team of 13.
→As more people submit, open Claude and say: "Read the moodboard-responses Slack channel and rebuild the dashboards." That's it.

The Biggest Pattern
We Have More to Say Than We're Saying
The most consistent thing across all 13 answers. Worth looking at together.

⚠️ 5 of us want more voice

Five people said some version of the same thing (and a sixth added a different angle: not wanting to lose the freshness of celebrating small wins as the role gets heavier): they want to be more direct, more confident, or more capable of communicating with the right people. Some of us are still in the early months of being here — building context, building trust, finding their voice in a new environment. That's entirely normal. The instincts are there across the board. What's developing — for different reasons for different people — is the confidence to back them fully.

💪 7 of us chose essential-but-invisible

Tomato sauce, mozzarella, cheese, caramelised onion — most of us described ourselves as the ingredient that holds everything together without being noticed. That's not false modesty. But it might be worth asking: what would it look like to be more visible about what we bring?

🔄 Under pressure, we lose altitude

Almost half the team said their support need is being zoomed out. When things get hard, we tend to dive into detail rather than stay connected to the goal. Knowing this about ourselves is already half of fixing it.

The Big Picture
What This All Says About Us
The team we actually are

We're a team with genuine analytical depth, strong structural discipline, and more calm than we give ourselves credit for. Six different working styles across 15 people — at different stages of their time here — means we cover almost every angle. The thinkers, the executors, the connectors, the energisers, the detail-catchers. That breadth is rare, and it gets richer as the newer members build their picture of who we are.

Our energy is spread across a healthy range. Most of us are managing well — and 3 in full orbit bringing real energy. One of us needs a win right now and the rest of us can help make that happen.

The thing most worth sitting with is the voice pattern. Five people want to say their thing more clearly and more confidently. We have the ideas. We have the instincts. What we're still building is the collective permission to back them fully — with stakeholders, with each other, in the moments that matter.

The hidden strengths in this team are real. Organisation, calm under pressure, the ability to hold complexity, the instinct to catch what others miss. These aren't small things. They're what makes delivery possible. The work now is making them visible — to each other, and to the people outside this room who benefit from them every day.
